Keep the drawer labeled

Types, free paths, and how money actually leaves the building.

“App that pays real money” is four different businesses glued together in App Store ads. This ledger splits them so you can review a listing in one pass: what kind of contest it is, whether USD is available without paying, and which identity stack sits between you and the prize.

Competition types

Free-path stamps

Free USD path

You can enter and, if you win, receive dollars without buying anything. Example: Kaggle featured competitions, ESPN and CBS pick’em, Dirty Dozen.

Free + AMOE

US sweepstakes law requires a no-purchase alternative. Sweeps casinos and Fliff give daily coins and a mail-in request for prize currency. Purchase only buys Gold Coins, never the prize coin itself.

Some free events

The product is mixed. Some Vocal challenges and Papaya gem rooms pay out at $0 entry; neighbouring items in the same lobby do not.

Cash needs entry fee

Papaya, Skillz, Pocket7. Free rooms exist, but they pay gems / Z / tickets. Real USD sits behind a cash buy-in. Listed so the catalog is honest, not so you deposit.

Verification stack

How prize money is obtained, in practice

  1. Win or redeem under the official rules. Chance drawings, skill score, judge panel, or sweeps-coin playthrough — the rules page says which.
  2. Be the person on the account. Name, age, and location must match. One account. VPNs get balances seized.
  3. Pass the operator’s check. Winner affidavit (ESPN, CBS, Fanatics), KYC photo ID (DFS operators), or W-9 / W-8BEN (Kaggle, Devpost, Topcoder, Vocal).
  4. Receive USD on a method in your name. PayPal, ACH, check, Stripe, or a gift card. Bonus currencies and Gold Coins do not withdraw.
  5. Keep the 1099. US persons generally report prizes. $600+ often triggers an information return. Taxes are yours, not the app’s.

Signup patterns we saw

  • iOS first: Papaya titles, Skillz Arena, Pocket7, Pick ’Em Sports, Sports Predictor, FFScout Mobile.
  • Browser first: ESPN, CBS, Sky Super 6, Vocal, Kaggle, Devpost, DrivenData, Topcoder, the FPL family, and the pool-hosting platforms.
  • Mail-in AMOE: Hand-written index card or #10 envelope, exact phrase, current postal code from the Sweeps Rules. Typed letters and photocopies are usually void.
